# Testing

An ad test is an experiment, and an experiment changes one thing. Most "tests" change five things and learn nothing.

## One element at a time

Pick the single element under test and hold everything else fixed: the same product, the same length, the same offer, the same setting, the same cut. If two things differ between variants, a result cannot be attributed to either.

## Test hooks first

The opening decides most of what happens next, so it is the highest-leverage variable and the cheapest to vary. Three to five versions of the first two seconds on an otherwise identical film is the first test worth running. Body, proof and call to action come after the hook is settled.

## What to hold constant

- The product and the offer.
- The length and the beat map.
- The setting, the person and the light.
- The call to action wording.
- The platform, placement and audience, as far as the user controls them.

## What to vary, in order

1. The hook: angle, then wording, then the visual event.
2. The proof: which fact, shown or said.
3. The order of beats: problem first, or result first.
4. The person: creator-led against product-only.
5. The length: the same idea at two lengths.

## Reading a result

- Decide the measure before the test, and the smallest difference that would change a decision. A number without a threshold is a story, not a result.
- Small samples move by chance. A variant that leads by a little on a few hundred views has not won yet.
- A better opening shows up as more people staying past the first seconds; a better body shows up later. Look at where the difference appears, not only the total.
- Confounds hide in time: a variant run on a different day, against a different audience, or after a price change is not comparable. Run variants together.
- Report the result as a finding with its limits: what changed, by how much, over how many, and what it does not prove.

## When nothing works

If several openings fail on the same film, the hook is probably not the problem. Check the proof and the promise: is there a fact a viewer would repeat to a friend? If not, the product story needs work before the ad does.

## Example plan, invented brand

Tessamund, the one-minute face cream. Last three ads opened with the jar on a shelf and did not hold viewers.

- Hold: the 15-second beat map, the time-lapse demonstration, the closing line, the audience.
- Vary: only beat one. Three openings: the wristwatch and the dab (demonstration), "still sticky an hour later?" (tension), the phone picked up with a clean hand (result first).
- Measure: the share of viewers still watching at three seconds, with a decision threshold agreed before the run.
- Then: the best opening becomes fixed, and the next test varies the proof.
