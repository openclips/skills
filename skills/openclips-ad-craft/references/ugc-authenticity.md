# What makes a creator video read as real

A creator video works because it does not look like an ad. The moment it does, it loses the one advantage it had. These are the tells, in both directions.

## The camera behaves like a phone

- Handheld, a little unsteady, held at arm's length or propped against something. Not a tripod, not a slider, not a drone.
- Vertical, close, eye level. The viewer is on a call with this person.
- One take at a time, with cuts where a person would naturally stop and restart. Not a seamless edit.
- Focus hunts for a moment when the product comes close. That is fine; it is what phones do.

## Only the room's own light

A window, a kitchen light, a bedside lamp, daylight in a car. Not a ring light with a perfect catchlight, not three-point lighting, not a colour wash. Name the light source in the brief so it is not invented.

## Facts, not adjectives

A real person says what happened: "it charged in about an hour", "it fits in the pocket of my jacket", "I have used it every morning for three weeks". They do not say "premium quality" or "game-changing". A brief with no facts gets a question back, not an adjective.

## Keep two imperfections

A slightly untidy background, a hair out of place, a word repeated, a laugh at a mistake, a pause to think. Pick two and keep them on purpose. A brief that asks for perfection produces something that looks like a commercial with the budget removed.

## Verbs that break the spell

"Introducing", "unlock", "elevate", "discover", "experience", "transform". Nobody talking to a friend uses them. Use the verbs of the situation: put, clip, fold, open, press, wait, forget, notice.

## Sensitive problems are implied, not performed

For products that solve something private or awkward, the creator does not act out the problem. They refer to it the way a friend would, briefly and without a face of disgust, and move on to what changed. Dwelling on the problem embarrasses the viewer and makes the creator look like an actor.

## No scripted sincerity

"I honestly love this", "I am not sponsored, I just really", "trust me". These lines say the opposite of what they mean. Sincerity is shown by specificity: the fact about the product only a user would know.

## The brief

A good creator brief is short: the product, the three facts to say in their own words, the setting and light, the two imperfections to keep, the one action to show on camera, and the length. It does not contain lines to read verbatim; it contains the things that must be true.

## Example brief, invented brand

Murmurlist, the notes app that turns a voice memo into a to-do list.

- Facts to say, in their own words: a memo of about ten seconds becomes a list; the list keeps the order you said things; it works with the phone in a pocket.
- Setting and light: walking to a bus stop, morning daylight, phone held up like a call.
- Action on camera: press record, say three errands, show the list appear.
- Keep: traffic noise, one restart.
- Length: fifteen seconds.
