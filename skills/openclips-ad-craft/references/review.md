# Reviewing a draft

A review is a list of findings, not a score. A score tells the author nothing to change; a finding does. An empty list is a valid result and should be given as one, not padded.

## How to review

1. Read the whole draft once without writing.
2. Check it against the brief: length, product facts, audience, call to action.
3. Walk the beats in time. For each, ask whether a viewer at that second knows why they are still watching.
4. Write each finding as: where (beat or shot), what is wrong, and one concrete change. Number them.
5. Order by damage: the finding that loses the most viewers first.
6. Stop. Do not add praise to balance the list, and do not add findings to make it look thorough.

## Common failure shapes

Check for each; report only the ones present.

- **Late hook.** Nothing happens in the first two seconds; the opening is an establishing shot, a greeting or a logo.
- **Two actions in one beat.** A beat asks the viewer to notice two things; one is lost.
- **Over budget.** The word count exceeds what the length can carry at a natural pace.
- **Adjective load.** Claims with no fact behind them: premium, amazing, effortless.
- **Rendered copy.** Text inside a frame that a model is expected to draw.
- **Product in motion, in a render.** A generated shot where the product spins, floats or morphs while the camera is still.
- **Camera indecision.** Two or more camera moves inside one shot.
- **Light from nowhere.** The description praises the lighting without saying where it comes from.
- **The wrong verb.** Introducing, unlock, elevate, discover, experience, transform.
- **Performed problem.** A sensitive problem acted out on camera instead of implied.
- **Missing proof.** The body asserts a benefit but never shows or states the fact that supports it.
- **Buried name.** The product name appears too early to matter, too briefly to be read, or not at all; the viewer leaves without it.
- **Sound-dependent.** The hook only works with audio on.
- **Cut-off ends.** Speech starts at zero or ends on the last frame.
- **Invented facts.** A number, feature or comparison the brief did not supply.

## Writing the finding

Bad: "The opening is weak."
Good: "1. Beat 1 (0 to 2 s): the first frame is the jar on a shelf, so nothing happens until the hand enters at 2.5 s. Open on the hand and the dab, with the watch already in frame."

## Example review, invented brand

Draft: a 15-second Tessamund script, forty-nine words, opening on the jar, a line saying "luxuriously lightweight", and the name spoken once over a moving final shot.

1. Beat 1 (0 to 2 s): the opening frame is the jar; the demonstration does not start until beat 2. Open on the dab and the second hand.
2. Word count: forty-nine words in fifteen seconds is over the natural pace. Cut beat 2 to one line.
3. Beat 2: "luxuriously lightweight" is two adjectives and no fact. Replace with the absorption time.
4. Beat 5: the name is spoken over a moving shot and never shown. Hold the jar still for the last second and put the name on the frame as composited text.
